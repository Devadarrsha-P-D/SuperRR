    Definition

    Pressure is how much the next ball can swing the match

    Required rate, wickets in hand and overs left interact: 12 an over is routine with eight wickets in hand and desperate with three. So we let a win-probability model combine them, then measure how much each ball can move it.

    Model

    Out-of-fold AUC for chases. Every ball is scored by a model that never saw its match. First innings reaches 0.68, so those balls count at half weight.

    Leverage
    Expected swing

    Apply each outcome (dot, 1 to 6, wicket) weighted by how often it happens in that phase, and average the absolute change in win probability.

    Live balls only
    3% to 97%

    Balls where the game is already decided are excluded from ratings. Among the rest, leverage is ranked within each innings and cut into four tiers.

    Pressure has a direction

    A batter is more pressured when his side is behind, a bowler when the batting side is ahead. A 50-50 game weighs both equally.

    batter weight = 0.6 + 0.8 × (1 − win prob)
    bowler weight = 0.6 + 0.8 × win prob

    Four tiers, one rule

    Leverage percentile within each innings, among live balls. Ratings weight balls by intensity, so High and Extreme balls drive the estimate, while Ordinary and Elevated balls anchor each player's usual level.

    Scoring

    Every ball is scored against its own situation

    Win probability only defines pressure. Players are scored in runs, because run value predicted next-season form where win-probability credit did not.

    1 · Expect
    State models

    Gradient-boosted models predict the runs, the wicket chance and the cost of a wicket for an average player in the same state: overs, wickets, target.

    2 · Value
    V per ball

    Runs above expected, minus the expected-runs cost of any wicket above its expected chance.

    3 · Rate
    Crossed ridge

    One model explains V with a batter effect and a bowler effect, plus batting slot and pace/spin. It controls for opponent and shrinks thin samples.

    4 · Check
    Bootstrap

    100 resamples of matches rerun the whole pipeline to give each player a probability of staying in the top 10.

    What goes into the rating

    Opponent. A batter is credited relative to the bowlers he faced, and a bowler relative to the batters he bowled to.
    Role. Batting slot (1-2, 3-4, 5-6, 7+) and pace versus spin are controlled for.
    Competition. Emerging Teams and Rising Stars matches count at half weight.
    Exclusions. Rain-affected matches, ties, no-results, super overs, run-outs and retirements.
    Shrinkage. Ridge penalties pull thin-sample players toward average. The typical player has only 15 to 25 balls in the top tiers.
    rating = baseline + shrunk pressure slope

    Read with care

    Where the model is less certain

    • Pressure-specific skill is hard to detect. Only baseline skill predicted future pressure results, so the ranking rests on that. We do not claim a measurable clutch gene.
    • Batters in the top tiers are the least predictable. Held-out correlation for batters in the High and Extreme tiers is near zero (+0.02). Read batter order within the top 10 as a tier. Bowlers are more reliable (+0.12).
    • Thin samples at the edge. Several players have fewer than 80 pressure balls. Shrinkage and the bootstrap account for it, but a few more matches could move them. Places 8 to 10 in both lists are close calls.
    • First-innings pressure is approximate. The first-innings win-probability model is weaker (AUC 0.68), so those balls count at half weight.
    • Missing context. Pitch, dew, partnerships and batting-order decisions are not modelled. Players who appear in only one competition are linked to others through shared opponents.
    • Settings were tuned on this data. Sensitivity checks show the effect is modest, but the final lists are not independently validated.
